The appellate opinion arrived without changing a single exhibit.

The opinion did not find that a student received a set. It did not identify the intended candidate, decide who created the original exercise, or determine whether the anonymous receipt was genuine. The judges also rejected the defense’s argument that the phrase “source key” could only refer to a document Ren had physically possessed. In context, they said, the trial court could interpret the signed request as a direction about what the placement office should include. That conclusion did not establish that Ren had seen the source table or knew every discrepancy in the exercise. It affirmed only the finding on the narrow count.

At the end, the opinion said the sealed appendix had been unnecessary to the decision and should be returned unopened if the clerk had not already reviewed it for filing compliance.
